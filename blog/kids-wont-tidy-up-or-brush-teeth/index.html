Kids Won't Tidy Up or Brush Teeth? 5 Ways to Get Started

When a child won't tidy up or brush their teeth, it's often because they've heard the same request from the same person too many times. A different approach, a different voice, or a few small steps can help. Here are 5 ideas I gathered while making Lion on the Line (有獅找你). You can try them without an app.

I'm Sona Orbit, the solo developer of Lion on the Line (有獅找你). The first five sections are ideas you can try without an app. The sixth introduces the app I made. Its features are described on this site as of November 2026. This article isn't medical or psychological advice. If you're concerned about your child, talk to a pediatrician or another professional.

1. Ask for help instead of giving an order

“Put away your toys” is an instruction. Your child hears that they're being told what to do. “The blocks need to go home. Can you help me put them in the box?” is a request. Your child hears that someone needs their help.

The task is the same, but the second version gives your child a role: helper. Many children respond better to a request for help than to an order.

2. Break it into steps they can see

“Clean your room” is a big task for a child, with no clear end in sight. Try “Put away 5 blocks first” or “Brush your top teeth.” They can see and count each step.

Each step counts as one. You don't need many. 3 to 5 is usually enough. When they're done, let the task be done. Don't add more at the last minute.

3. Let someone else ask

After hearing the same thing from a parent ten times, a child may tune it out. Another voice can help: a stuffed animal, another adult at home, or a character they like.

What matters is that the voice is different and the tone is a request, not a scolding. A stuffed animal asking, “My house is full of blocks. Can you help me?” may work better than an adult saying “Put them away” for the eleventh time.

4. Respond to each step

When your child completes a step, respond right away in a way they can see or hear. “Wow, the first block is home!” can mean more than waiting until everything is put away to say “Great job.”

Respond to what they did. You don't need a gift. A clap, a cheer, or a circle drawn on paper will do.

5. Skip threats and bargains

“No cartoons unless you clean up” or “I'll give you candy when you're done” may work for now. But a child can quickly learn to treat every task as a trade. Next time, they may ask, “What do I get?”

Instead, give the task a good ending: when it's done, someone thanks your child, and that's it. If they really don't want to do it today, you can let them say “Later” and try again at another time rather than turning it into a standoff.

6. Let a Lion on the Line character call

I built the ideas in sections 1 through 5 into Lion on the Line. A parent picks a character from the animal village and a task. The character then “calls” the child to ask for help.

  • At the setup screen, pick a character and task, such as Sheriff Leo and “Tidy up toys.” Then choose how many times to do it (1 to 5) and when the phone should ring.
  • When the phone rings, the child taps “Answer.” The character explains what they need help with.
  • Each time the child completes a step, you press once. The character cheers on the call, and a small object for that task appears on screen. For tidying toys, it's a block. When all the objects are there, the character says thank you and hangs up.
  • Ten characters each have a task: eating, tidying toys, getting ready to go out, packing a school bag, taking a bath, washing hands, getting ready for bed, settling down, doing homework, brushing teeth, and getting dressed.
  • You can use one phone, or have another iPhone or iPad on the same Wi-Fi ring.

The lines are written in advance. They don't scare or threaten the child, or promise a reward. If the child taps “Later,” the call ends without a scolding.

It isn't a real call. You and your child can't hear each other through the app. It doesn't use a microphone or camera, and you don't need an account. It requires iOS 17 or later. Lion on the Line is being prepared for release. You can find more details on the website.

7. Frequently asked questions

What if my child says “Later” every time?

Accept “Later” first, then agree on a specific time to try again, such as “After this episode ends.” Insisting that it happen right away every time can turn it into a struggle.

How many times should they do the task?

3 to 5 is usually enough. If there are too many steps, your child may not see the end and may give up partway through.

Will Lion on the Line characters say my child's name?

No. The lines and audio are recorded in advance. Characters call the child “little one.”

Does Lion on the Line need an internet connection?

The first time you use it, you need to download a character voice pack (about 5–6 MB). After that, calls don't need an internet connection. If you haven't downloaded a voice pack, the app uses your phone's built-in voice.
